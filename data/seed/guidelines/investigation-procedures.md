# Investigation Procedures (Synthetic Prototype Excerpt)

> Prototype Dataset — Synthetic Data. Paraphrased procedural guidance for
> demonstration purposes only.

## Opening a Review

A review case is opened when risk indicators, complaints, audit observations, or
inspection findings suggest a work requires closer examination. The officer
opening the case records the triggering grounds. Opening a review is a
scrutiny step; it does not imply any finding against any person or agency.

## Evidence Standards

Every conclusion recorded in a case must reference verifiable records: sanction
documents, measurement books, payment vouchers, treasury transactions,
inspection reports, or corrected data. AI-generated indicators may guide where
to look but are not themselves evidence of wrongdoing. Conclusions must
distinguish between verified facts, explanations received, and open questions.

## Escalation

A case is escalated when verified evidence indicates a potential irregularity
beyond the reviewing officer's authority to resolve: unexplained missing funds
flow, confirmed double funding, or material misreporting. Escalation transfers
the matter to the competent authority with the documented evidence; the
reviewing officer records what was verified and what remains open.

## Recording Outcomes

Each closed case records one outcome with written reasons: a verified issue
requiring action, no issue found, a data correction, escalation, or an
inconclusive result with the specific information that was unavailable.
Outcome language must remain factual and neutral; determinations of fraud,
corruption, or personal guilt are made only by competent authorities through
due process, never by the monitoring system.

## Time Standards

Higher-priority cases should be reviewed first and completed within the
prescribed period for their priority class. Where a case waits on information
from another office, the request date and the awaited information are recorded
so delays are attributable and recoverable.
